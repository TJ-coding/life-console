## What's on the page

- **Top 3.** The three tasks that would make today a good day if nothing else got done, each with its minutes.
- **Everything else.** Twelve lines for the rest, with minutes and a tick box.
- **Workload bar.** Seven boxes, one per hour of planned work. Shade one for each hour you plan; if you need an eighth, the day is too full.
- **Schedule.** Every hour from 05:00 to 22:00, with half-hour lines, to block tasks into.
- **Notes and evening review:** *Done today* and *Moves to tomorrow*.

## How to time block a day with it

1. **The night before or first thing, fill in the top 3.** Write the minutes next to each. If you can only name one, that's fine.
2. **List everything else** you'd like to get to, with honest minutes.
3. **Shade the workload bar** as you add up the minutes. Stop at 7 hours. Whatever doesn't fit goes on another day now, not at 6 pm.
4. **Block the schedule.** Fixed things first (meetings, classes, the school run), then the top 3 in your best hours, then the rest. Leave a gap or two for what nobody planned.
5. **In the evening,** tick what's done and write what moves to tomorrow. That list is tomorrow's starting point.

## Why only 7 hours?

A working day is never 8 or 9 hours of tasks: there are messages, breaks, interruptions and tasks that run long. Planning about 7 hours of real work, and moving the rest before the day starts, is what makes the plan one you finish. If your days are shorter, cross out the boxes you don't have.
