# Todoist alternatives for planning your day

<p class="meta">Prices and features checked 23 November 2026 on each app's own website. Written by the developer of Life Console, which is listed first; the others are listed for what they do better.</p>

<p class="summary">Todoist is an excellent list: fast capture, projects, filters and 120+ integrations. People usually look for an alternative for one of three reasons: they want to <b>plan when tasks happen</b>, not just list them (the calendar layout and task durations are Pro features, $5 a month billed yearly or $7 monthly); they've hit the free plan's <b>5-project limit</b>; or they want an app that's <b>open source or on their own server</b>. Pick by which of those it is.</p>

| | Best for | Price | Runs on | Open source / self-host |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Life Console** | Turning a list into a time-blocked week, linked to goals and deadlines | Free | Browser (installs on phone and desktop, works offline); Mac menu-bar app | Yes (AGPL-3.0), Docker |
| **TickTick** | Todoist's features plus a calendar, habits and Pomodoro, cheaper | Free; Premium $49.99 a year | Phone, computer, tablet, watch | No |
| **Things 3** | A beautiful list on Apple devices | Paid, per platform, on the App Store; free trial | Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Vision Pro | No |
| **Super Productivity** | Focused solo work with time tracking | Free | Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, web | Yes (MIT), optional self-hosted sync |
| **Vikunja** | Todoist-style projects on your own server, shared with others | Free to self-host; paid cloud | Browser, self-hosted server | Yes (AGPL-3.0), Docker or binary |

## 1. Life Console

![Plan week in Life Console: backlog, a 7-hour workload bucket per day and the day's timeline](/media/week@2x.webp)

For when your Todoist list is long but your week is still unplanned. Tasks start in a **backlog**, then **Plan week** gives every day a 7-hour bucket: drop tasks into a day, then onto its timeline, or press **Auto time-box** to fill free gaps by priority. Each task can link to a long-term goal (`@SIG-01.2`) and a deadline, so the plan shows why a task matters as well as when it's due. Quick-add parses a line the way Todoist does: `Draft report 90m due:fri p2`.

- **Free and open source:** hosted at life-console.dev, or self-host it with Docker Compose ([guide](/docs/self-hosting)).
- **Lacks:** Todoist's integrations, shared projects and native phone apps (it installs from the browser). There's no Todoist importer yet.

## 2. TickTick

The closest like-for-like switch. It has lists, natural-language dates, reminders and Kanban like Todoist, plus a calendar with a weekly view of busy and free time, habits, an Eisenhower matrix and a built-in Pomodoro timer.

- **Pick it** if you want Todoist plus a calendar in one cheap app.
- **Costs:** free with limits (9 lists, 99 tasks per list, a basic calendar view); Premium is $49.99 a year and adds the timeline view, task durations and more calendar views.

## 3. Things 3

A carefully designed task manager for planning the day and managing projects, on Apple devices only. It's sold through the App Store for each platform, with Apple setting the price in your currency, and there's a free trial to download first.

- **Pick it** if you're all-Apple and want the most polished list.
- **Lacks:** Windows, Android and web apps.

## 4. Super Productivity

A free, open-source (MIT) task manager built for one person's focused work. It adds what Todoist lacks for deep work: a schedule next to your calendar events, timeboxing, Pomodoro and time tracking with timesheet export. It pulls tasks from Jira, GitHub, GitLab, Linear, ClickUp and others, and syncs through SuperSync (which you can host yourself), Dropbox or WebDAV.

- **Pick it over Life Console** if you need time logs, or your work lives in issue trackers.

## 5. Vikunja

The most direct **self-hosted Todoist alternative**: open source (AGPL-3.0), with list, Kanban, Gantt and table views, sharing with other people, and a **built-in Todoist importer** (also Trello and Microsoft To Do). Run it with Docker or a single binary, or pay for its hosted cloud.

- **Pick it** if you want your Todoist projects, as they are, on your own server.
- **Lacks:** a day timeline or planning ritual: it's a task and project manager.

## Can you time block in Todoist?

Partly. On the free plan, a task can have a date and a time but no length, and there's no calendar layout. **Todoist Pro** ($5 a month billed yearly, $7 monthly) adds **task durations** and the **calendar layout**, so you can see tasks laid out on a day and week, and connects up to 3 calendars (1 on the free plan) so your events show next to your tasks.

That's enough if your tasks are few and your days are mostly fixed. What Todoist doesn't do is tell you when a day holds more work than hours, or move what slipped. If time blocking is the reason you're looking, compare [the best time blocking apps →](/alternatives/time-blocking-apps), or see [how to time block in Google Calendar](/guides/google-calendar-time-blocking) for free.

## Which one?

- Your list is fine but your week isn't planned → **Life Console**
- Todoist plus a calendar and habits, for less → **TickTick**
- All-Apple and want polish → **Things 3**
- Time tracking for focused work, free → **Super Productivity**
- Move your Todoist projects to your own server → **Vikunja** (it imports them)
- You depend on Todoist's integrations or team projects → stay with Todoist Pro or Business
