Know what to do right now.

Life Console turns your goals into a week plan, and your week plan into the next thing to do. Keyboard-first and free; use it here or run it on your own server. Free, works offline, and installs on your phone.

Free and open source, no ads. Sign in with Google; there's no password to make up.

Life Console started as a set of paper planning sheets: a week plan with a backlog, a 7-hour budget for each day and a timeline, a deadline list, and a strategic plan running from vision down to milestones. It joins those sheets into one console, so the answer to “what should I be doing now?” is always on screen.

S-01 Command Center

The current block, counting down.

The first thing you see is the block you should be in, how long is left, and the task to work on. Below that: today's timeline, the next deadline and what's left to do today.

  • The countdown also sits in the top bar and the browser tab title.
  • In free time, it suggests the highest-priority task that fits before your next block.

S-10 Plan week

Plan the week in three moves.

Backlog → day → time. Give each task a day, and its estimate fills that day's 7-hour bucket. Red overflow means the day is too full. Then drag tasks onto the timeline, or press Auto time-box to fit them into free gaps between 08:00 and 21:00 by priority.

  • A big task's estimate becomes a budget you spread over several days.
  • With Pomodoro on, plans move in whole pomodoros and the breaks are laid out for you.
  • Every change can be undone.

S-30 Goal flow

See what each goal is waiting on.

Every plan draws itself as a flow on a timeline. Milestones feed the goals that depend on them, goals feed the vision, and each card sits on its end date. Click a goal to trace everything it needs; drag a card to move its date.

Open source

Your planner, on your server.

Life Console is free software under the GNU AGPL-3.0. life-console.dev runs the same code you can read on GitHub. If you'd rather keep your plans on your own machine, three commands start it.

It needs Docker with the compose plugin. Open http://localhost:8080 and create an account; the first one becomes the admin. To install it on phones and get notifications, put it behind HTTPS (Caddy, Traefik, nginx or a Cloudflare Tunnel) and set APP_URL.

Read the self-hosting guide →

$ git clone https://github.com/TJ-coding/life-console.git
$ cd life-console && cp .env.example .env
$ docker compose up -d
# PostgreSQL + app + backups on :8080
Runs as
Three containers from one docker-compose.yml: PostgreSQL 17, the app (Node 22) and a backup job. About 300 MB of RAM.
Backups
A database dump every 24 hours, kept for 14 days by default, plus a dump before every schema migration. Per-account snapshots let you roll a planner back.
Updates
Prebuilt images on GitHub Container Registry. Follow latest, or pin a build with LC_VERSION and roll back by changing it.
Accounts
Email and password, Google sign-in, or both. Close sign-ups with SIGNUPS=closed and add people from the admin CLI.
Notifications
Web Push from your own server. The keys are generated on first start, so no third-party account is needed.
Privacy
No analytics. A self-hosted server tells search engines to stay out.

S-20 Todo inbox

Add a task in one line.

Write the estimate, due date, priority and goal link straight into the title. The parser shows what it understood as you type.

  • Subtasks to any depth, each taking a share of the parent's time budget.
  • Filter by day, goal, type or priority, and edit many tasks at once.

S-21 Deadlines

Deadlines as time left, not dates.

Each deadline shows how long is left, coloured by urgency, grouped into overdue, today, this week, next week and later, beside a month calendar. Set a personal target date to finish ahead of the real one.

Reminders arrive as push notifications with a Mark done button.

S-30 Strategy

Tasks that know which goal they serve.

Write a plan from a vision down to goals and milestones, each with success criteria and a time bound. Tag a task @SIG-01.2 and it appears under that milestone.

  • The next milestone is shown as a countdown.
  • Late milestones turn red, and the next one is marked.

S-01F Focus

One key to hide everything else.

Press F. The screen shows the current block, a large timer and your top three actions. Tick them off with 1 2 3 and leave with Esc.

Phone and offline

Install it from the browser.

It installs on Android, iPhone and desktop, works without a connection, and syncs when you're back. If two devices edit at the same time, both changes are kept.

On a phone the sidebar becomes a tab bar, and a lock stops you dragging the schedule by accident while you scroll.

FAQ

Is Life Console open source?

Yes. The code is on GitHub under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0. You can use, change and self-host it. If you run a modified version as a public service, you must share your changes under the same license.

Is it free?

Yes. The hosted service at life-console.dev is free and has no ads. Self-hosting is free too. If it helps you, you can buy me a coffee to keep it running.

What do I need to self-host it?

A machine with Docker and the compose plugin, and about 300 MB of RAM. It runs as three containers: PostgreSQL, the app and a backup job. Put an HTTPS reverse proxy in front of it to install it on phones and get push notifications.

Can I move between the hosted service and my own server?

Yes. Export your planner as JSON from Settings and import it on the other server.

How do I sign in on life-console.dev?

With Google. The hosted service doesn't store passwords. A self-hosted server can use email and password, Google, or both. The privacy policy lists what's stored.

Does it work on my phone and offline?

Yes. It installs from the browser on Android, iPhone and desktop, keeps working offline, and syncs when you're back online.

Does it support Pomodoro?

Yes. With Pomodoro on (25-minute work, 5-minute short and 15-minute long breaks by default), plans move in whole pomodoros and the breaks are laid out on the timeline.
